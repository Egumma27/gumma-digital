Websites & landing pages

The site your business is judged by. Hand-built rather than assembled from plugins, so it loads in under a second, survives a redesign, and costs almost nothing to host.

Mobile apps

One codebase for iOS and Android, or fully native where the platform genuinely matters. I handle the parts that usually stall a launch — signing, store listings, and review rejections.

Rescue & maintenance

An inherited codebase nobody understands, a site that keeps breaking, or a developer who disappeared. I'll audit what's there, tell you plainly whether to fix or rebuild, and then do it.

  • Codebase audit with a written verdict
  • Performance and security fixes
  • Dependency and platform upgrades
  • Documentation so the next person copes

Questions

The things people ask first

How do payments work?
Half up front to book the slot, half on delivery. Longer projects are split into milestones so you're never paying far ahead of the work. Retainers are billed monthly.
What if I don't know exactly what I want yet?
That's normal, and it's what the discovery call is for. Most people arrive with a problem rather than a spec. We'll turn it into one together — and if the honest answer is that you don't need custom software, I'll tell you that.
Who owns the code and the accounts?
You do, entirely, once the final invoice is paid. Code sits in your repository, hosting and domains go in your name, and you get the handover documentation. Nothing is held hostage.
What happens if the scope changes mid-project?
Small adjustments are part of building something well and I don't nickel and dime them. Anything genuinely new gets quoted separately before it starts, so the original timeline and price stay honest.
Do you work with clients in other time zones?
Yes. Work happens asynchronously with a written update and a demo each week, plus a call whenever one is genuinely useful. Overlap of a couple of hours is plenty.
Can you take over a project someone else started?
Often, yes. It starts with a paid audit — a few days spent reading the code and infrastructure — after which you get a written recommendation on whether to continue it, refactor it, or start again. The audit fee comes off the project if you go ahead.
